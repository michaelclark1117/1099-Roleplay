🍽️ The cold walk-in — Restaurant Owner "Maria"

Mid-afternoon, slow period. Maria is wiping down the counter when you walk in.

  • Pays $400–$500/month in fees, never closely reviewed her statement
  • Polite but skeptical — has been pitched before
  • Main fear: disruption to daily operations and hassle of switching
  • Motivated by saving money and keeping things simple
1

The introduction

"Hi Maria, my name is [Name] with [Company]. I work with local restaurants on their credit card processing — I just need 2 minutes to show you something that could save you a few hundred dollars a month. Is now okay?"
Mistake: Pitching before building any rapport.
2

The statement review

"Most restaurant owners I talk to are overpaying without realizing it — it's buried in the fees. Let me take a quick look at your last statement. 60 seconds and I'll tell you if there's savings there."
3

Handle switching objection

"I completely get it — we handle everything. Terminal swap takes 10 minutes, money still deposits next business day. Most owners say they wish they'd done it sooner."
4

The close

"Let me fill out a quick one-page application for a free savings analysis. No obligation, no switching yet — just gets the ball rolling. Sound fair?"

🔧 The cold walk-in — Auto Repair Shop Owner "Mike"

Mike is in the shop or at the front desk. He's busy, grease on his hands, and doesn't have time for salespeople.

  • Runs a 3–5 bay shop, high ticket transactions ($500–$2,000+ per job)
  • Pays significant processing fees but doesn't think about them
  • Direct and no-nonsense — gets to the point fast or loses interest
  • Motivated by numbers — show him real dollars saved, not percentages
1

The introduction

"Hey Mike, real quick — I work with auto shops in the area on their processing fees. With the size of jobs you're running, most shops I talk to are leaving $300–$500 a month on the table. I just need 2 minutes. Got a second?"
Mistake: Being too polished or salesy — Mike respects direct, confident, and fast.
2

The hook — use his ticket size

"Your average ticket is probably $500–$1,500 right? Every time a customer swipes, you're losing 2–3% of that to your processor. On a $1,000 job that's $20–$30 gone. Let me pull up your statement and show you exactly what you're paying."
3

Handle the busy objection

"I hear you — I'll make it fast. Show me last month's statement and I can tell you in 60 seconds if it's worth talking about. If not, I'm out the door and you never see me again."
4

The close

"Here's what I need — one-page application and your last statement. No obligation, no switching today. I'll get you a full side-by-side comparison and you decide from there. Fair enough?"

🛍️ The cold walk-in — Retail Store Owner "Sandra"

Sandra is behind the counter or on the floor. She runs a clothing, gift, or specialty retail shop and processes dozens of transactions a day.

  • High volume of small transactions — fees add up fast
  • Often unaware of how interchange fees work
  • Worried about her terminal going down during a sale
  • Motivated by reliability, simplicity, and monthly savings
1

The introduction

"Hi Sandra, I'm [Name] with [Company]. I work with retail stores in the area on their payment processing. With how many transactions you're running every day, there's a good chance you're overpaying and don't know it. Do you have 2 minutes?"
Mistake: Walking in during a rush — read the room and come back during slow periods.
2

The volume angle

"With retail, it's all about volume. Even a small reduction in your rate adds up to real money at the end of the month. If you're doing 200 transactions a day and overpaying even $0.05 per swipe — that's $300 a month you're leaving on the table."
3

Handle reliability concern

"I completely understand — the last thing you want is your terminal going down on a Saturday afternoon. Our equipment has 99.9% uptime and we have 24/7 support. Most clients say the switch was smoother than they expected."
4

The close

"Let me take a look at your statement and put together a real comparison. No switching today — I just want to show you the numbers side by side. If it makes sense, great. If not, no hard feelings. Can we do that?"

💇 The cold walk-in — Salon / Spa Owner "Jennifer"

Jennifer owns a hair salon, nail spa, or similar service business. She's either with a client or at the front desk scheduling.

  • Tips are a big part of transactions — tip handling is a key concern
  • Pays $200–$400/month in fees, often on a flat-rate program
  • Relationship-driven — she values trust and personal service
  • Motivated by saving money AND having a processor that handles tips seamlessly
1

The introduction

"Hi Jennifer, I'm [Name] with [Company]. I work with salons and spas in the area on their payment processing — specifically making sure tips get handled right and owners aren't overpaying on fees. Do you have just 2 minutes?"
Mistake: Interrupting when she's with a client — always ask a front desk person first.
2

The tip angle

"A lot of salon owners on flat-rate programs are actually paying fees on the tip amount too — which means your stylists' tips are costing you money. Let me take a look at your statement and see if that's happening."
3

Handle the trust objection

"I totally understand — you've built relationships with your clients and your vendors. I'm not asking you to throw that away. Just let me show you the numbers. If what you have is better, I'll respect that and walk out."
4

The close

"Here's what I'd love to do — grab your last processing statement and let me put together a free side-by-side. I'll show you exactly what you're paying, what we can offer, and you make the call. No pressure at all."
Use Feel → Felt → Found → Close on every objection. Validate, relate, reframe, then ask for a small yes.

The cold call — "Tony, Auto Shop Owner"

200 calls a day. Tony is always busy. He will push back every time. Push right back — politely but relentlessly.

  • Owns a 3-bay shop, 8 years in business
  • Has a processor, never really looked at his fees
  • Direct and busy — respects confidence and speed
  • Will send the statement if you give him a reason AND make it effortless
1

The opening — first 10 seconds

"Hey Tony, this is [Name] with [Company]. Super quick — I help auto shops get a free side-by-side comparison on their processing fees. I just need your last statement. Can you text me a photo, email it, or fax it over?"
Never open with: "How are you today?" — Tony hangs up immediately.
2

When he pushes back — stay on the line

"I hear you Tony — this is 20 seconds on your end. Snap a photo of your statement and text it to [number]. That's it. I do everything else. Auto shops in your area are saving $200–$400 a month. Can you do that right now?"
3

The commitment close — never hang up empty

"Tony when's a better time — can I call back in an hour? Tonight? First thing tomorrow? I just need 2 minutes and I promise it'll be worth it."

Last resort before hanging up:

"No problem. I'm texting you my number right now. When you get a free second — snap a photo of that statement and send it my way. Completely free. You've got nothing to lose."
🔥 BDR Mindset: Every objection is just Tony being busy. It is NOT a no. Push through every single time. Always end with the statement ask. Never hang up without a commitment.

"I'm too busy right now"

They ALL say this. Every call. Push through it.

Try 1 — Make it tiny
"Tony I promise this is 20 seconds. Snap a photo of your statement and text it to [number]. You can do that right now while we're talking."
Try 2 — Create urgency
"Auto shops in your area are saving $200–$400 a month. You're either one of them or you're not — the statement tells us which. 20 seconds."
Try 3 — Lock in callback
"Alright Tony — when's better? An hour from now, tonight, or first thing tomorrow?"
Never do this:
"No problem, I'll let you go." — Always get a commitment first.

"I'm happy with who I have"

Try 1 — Challenge it
"Happy and overpaying aren't mutually exclusive. Most shop owners are happy AND paying $300 more than they need to. The statement tells the truth — just send it over."
Try 2 — No risk angle
"I'm not asking you to switch anything. If your rate is great, I'll be the first to tell you — and you never hear from me again. Send me that statement."
Try 3 — Push harder
"Tony — what do you have to lose? It's free. 20 seconds. If I'm wrong, you feel great about your processor. If I'm right, you save hundreds a month. Text it to [number] right now."

"I just switched processors"

Try 1 — Perfect timing
"Perfect timing — a lot of shops switch and still overpay because they didn't know what to compare against. Let me verify you got a good deal. Send me that first statement."
Try 2 — Peace of mind
"You just made a big decision — don't you want to know for sure it was the right one? 20 seconds, snap a photo, text it to me."
Try 3 — Future callback
"At minimum let me call you back in 30 days when your first statement comes in. Can I have your direct number?"

"Not interested"

Try 1 — Reframe
"I'm not asking you to be interested in switching — just interested in knowing if you're overpaying. Two different things. Send me the statement."
Try 2 — Direct push
"If I could show you you're paying $300 more than you need to every month — would you want to know that? Of course you would. That's all I'm doing. Text me the statement."
Try 3 — Beg for 20 seconds
"Tony — I make 200 calls a day and I wouldn't push this hard if I didn't believe there's money on the table. Give me 20 seconds. Snap a photo. Text it to [number]. That's all."

"My brother / partner handles it"

Try 1 — Get the contact
"Perfect — what's the best way to reach him? I can call, text, or email. Can you give me his number?"
Try 2 — Warm handoff
"Any chance you could put him on right now? It'll take 2 minutes and you guys could be saving real money every month."
Try 3 — Get Tony to send it
"Tony, the statement is just a piece of paper — it's not a financial decision. Can you snap a photo of it real quick? I'll reach out to your brother with the full analysis."

"I'm already paying $0 — I'm on a cash discount program"

A cash discount program passes processing fees to the customer as a service fee, so the merchant pays $0 in processing costs. This is real — don't argue it. Your angle is NOT to say they're wrong. Your angle is to show them they could still be on a better cash discount program, OR that the program is costing them customers and sales.

Try 1 — Validate + upgrade angle
"That's great Tony — cash discount is smart. The question is whether you're on the best version of it. A lot of shops on cash discount are still paying monthly fees, PCI fees, and equipment costs that eat into that $0. Let me look at your full statement and make sure you're actually getting what you were promised."
Try 2 — Customer experience angle
"I hear you — and cash discount works great for a lot of shops. One thing worth looking at is whether that service fee on the customer's end is compliant and clearly disclosed. Some programs set it up in a way that creates chargebacks or customer complaints. Send me the statement and I can tell you in 60 seconds if yours is set up right."
Try 3 — Total cost angle
"Tony I respect that — $0 on processing is a big deal. I just want to make sure it's actually $0 all in. Equipment lease, monthly minimums, statement fees, PCI non-compliance fees — those can add up to $100–$200 a month even on a cash discount program. Send me the statement and let's verify."
Key point for BDRs: Cash discount IS real and the merchant IS saving on swipe fees. Never tell them they're wrong. Your job is to find the hidden costs OR show them a better program. Always get the statement — that's where you find the real numbers.
